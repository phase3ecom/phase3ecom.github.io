02
Distinct Clusters
02

Grouped by underlying strategic logic — not surface similarity. Each cluster represents a genuinely different emotional or rational trigger.

A
THREAT / DEFENCE
Angles 1, 2, 18, 27, 28
Something bad is happening to your brand right now without your knowledge or permission. The action is reactive, not aspirational. Most emotionally urgent.
B
IDENTITY / BUILDER'S PRIDE
Angles 7, 16, 26
You built something real — why would you let someone else control how it shows up? Speaks to ownership, not ROI. Bypasses the rational objection loop.
C
PLATFORM RISK HEDGE
Angles 3, 30
Your current growth engine (Meta, Google, Shopify SEO) can be taken from you overnight — Amazon is structural diversification. Speaks to the paranoid operator.
D
DATA / INTELLIGENCE PLAY
Angles 9, 17, 20
Amazon PPC isn't an ad cost — it's a purchase of real buyer search data that makes every other channel more effective. Reframes the entire value proposition.
E
ASSET / EXIT VALUE
Angles 11, 29
Amazon presence is a compounding asset that increases business sale price — not a marketing expense. Speaks to the owner building to sell.
F
FIRST-MOVER / GEOGRAPHY
Angles 15, 21
Amazon AU is still early-stage. Cost to get in is low now. The window is measurably closing. Urgency that's earned, not manufactured.
G
PROOF OF CONCEPT / SINGLE SKU
Angle 23
Don't commit to the whole thing — test one product, 90 days, real numbers. Removes the biggest psychological barrier (commitment without certainty).
H
ANTI-AGENCY POSITIONING
Angles 24, 25
We're not selling you Amazon services — we're solving the 'should I worry about Amazon' problem so you can stop worrying about it.
I
SECONDARY CHANNEL BENEFITS
Angles 12, 22, 26
Amazon does things Shopify can't: public indexed reviews, B2B buyers, social proof for press/stockists. Not about direct sales — about what Amazon unlocks elsewhere.
J
SEASONAL / TIMING
Angle 5
There is a right time and a wrong time to set up on Amazon. If you miss May/June, Q4 setup is chaotic and expensive.
03
Standout Angles Worth Exploring
03

The 'Accidental Arbitrage' (Angle 18)

If a reseller is buying your product from your Shopify store and relisting it on Amazon at a markup — they are extracting a channel's worth of margin from your business, and you're funding it. This happens. The solution is not just 'get on Amazon' — it's 'take back control of your own supply chain economics.' This angle is uncomfortable because it implies the business owner has been asleep. That discomfort is the point.

Amazon as Exit Strategy (Angles 11 + 29)

No competitor is talking about Amazon presence in terms of business valuation. A brand with a controlled, reviewed, ranked Amazon presence has a demonstrably higher exit multiple than one without. For any owner thinking about selling in the next 3–5 years, this reframes the $1k/month retainer as an investment in asset value, not a marketing cost.

Benchmark Your Category — Live (Angle 20)

Run this as part of the sales call: 'Tell me your top 3 competitors.' Then show exactly how many are on Amazon, which are running PPC, what their reviews look like, and how the category ranks for search volume. Converts the abstract threat into something specific and visible. No competitor does this as a free diagnostic.

04
Obvious Ones to Rule Out
04

These are the default agency messages. Every competitor uses them. They're ignored, distrusted, or actively filtered out by the audience.

“Amazon is growing fast in Australia”
Every agency says this. Ignored.
“More channels = more revenue”
Proven ineffective in audience research. Rationalised away by fees/complexity.
“We handle everything so you don't have to”
Every DFY service says this. No differentiation.
“Free consultation / free audit” (on its own)
Table stakes without a specific hook. Creates no urgency.
“Our clients see X% growth”
Unverifiable, distrusted, every agency claims it.
