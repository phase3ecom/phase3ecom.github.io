01
Assumptions Identified
01

Before building strategy, list every assumption embedded in the original brief. These are the beliefs we've accepted without proof.

01
Business owners want more revenue / more sales channels
Partially true, but at what cost? Most small ecom owners are already overwhelmed. More revenue that creates more complexity isn’t actually wanted. What they want is more profit with less stress.
02
Done-for-you saves time
Only valuable if they believe the outcome is real. DFY is not a desire — it’s a delivery mechanism. The desire is underneath it.
03
Amazon is an opportunity they’re missing
This assumes they’re thinking about Amazon at all. Many aren’t. They’re thinking about their Shopify conversion rate, their ad costs, their margins. Amazon isn’t even in the frame.
04
Complexity is the barrier
The research says they cite complexity. But complexity is a rationalisation. The real barrier is: ‘I don’t believe it will work for me specifically.’ Complexity is the excuse, not the reason.
05
If they understood it better, they’d want it
This assumes education = conversion. It doesn’t. Understanding Amazon’s opportunity doesn’t resolve the personal risk calculation.
06
$1k/month is affordable
For a business doing $20k/month revenue with 30% margins, $1k/month is 17% of net profit. That’s not small. ROI must be clear and fast.
07
Shopify sellers see Amazon as additive
The research shows they see it as threatening (cannibalisation, margin erosion). This assumption is directly contradicted by the audience data.
08
These owners are growth-minded
Some are. Many are in survival mode, optimising for margin not growth.
09
PPC is the ongoing value
This assumes ongoing management is what they’ll pay for. Actually they may want to be shown it works once, then take it in-house or leave.
10
Agency selling a service
This frames it as a vendor relationship. But the research shows these owners want a trusted advisor who is accountable to results, not a service provider accountable to deliverables.
02
Base Truths
02

Strip every assumption. What’s left is what we know for certain — the foundation everything else must be built on.

1
Every ecom business owner’s core goal is: more profit, less personal risk, less stress — in that order.
2
Amazon AU has 60% Australian buyer penetration. It is a real, large, existing market.
3
These business owners do NOT have time, expertise, or risk appetite to figure out Amazon themselves.
4
Their primary fear is not complexity — it’s: spending money and getting nothing back.
5
Competitors and/or unauthorised resellers may already occupy their brand space on Amazon without them.
6
$1k/month is meaningful money for a small ecom business. It must produce visible return, fast.
7
Trust precedes purchase. These owners will not pay a stranger to manage a channel they don’t understand.
8
Amazon discovery does demonstrably drive traffic back to owned channels (48% stat). This is verifiable.
03
Rebuild from Base Truths
03

Starting only from the base truths — with no agency framing — what is this business owner actually looking for?

MOTIVATION 01 — FEAR REMOVAL
Strongest motivator

‘Someone I trust is handling this, so I don’t have to worry about it.’ The purchase is not about Amazon. It’s about peace of mind and eliminating a perceived threat — competitors on their brand terms, resellers undercutting them.

MOTIVATION 02 — PROOF OF CONCEPT
Reduces commitment barrier

‘Show me this works for a business like mine before I commit to anything ongoing.’ The setup fee is the real sale. The retainer is only bought after trust is established by results.

MOTIVATION 03 — COMPETITIVE DEFENCE
Most actionable trigger

‘If I’m not there, someone else is filling that space.’ This is the most actionable motivator. Not opportunity — threat. Not FOMO on growth — fear of losing ground they didn’t know they had.

THE PIVOT
Not ‘Amazon is a growth channel.’ But: ‘Your brand is either controlled on Amazon, or it isn’t. Right now, you don’t control it. We fix that.’

Recommended Actions
# Action Why
1 Reframe the lead message from opportunity to threat Your brand is probably already on Amazon. The question is whether you control it.
2 Position the setup fee as a risk audit + fix, not a launch service Reframes cost as protection, not investment in an unknown
3 Make the retainer conditional on setup results Entry is earned, not assumed. Reduces commitment anxiety.
4 Sell to the business owner’s identity, not their growth goals These are people who built something. Amazon threatening their brand is an attack on what they built.
5 For Audience 2: the pitch is not ‘PPC works’ It’s ‘here’s why it didn’t work the first time, and what’s different now.’
