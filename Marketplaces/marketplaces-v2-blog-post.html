The real cost of not being on Amazon: what Australian Shopify brands are losing every month

12/05/2026  ·  Phase 3 Ecom

Most Australian Shopify brands know they should be on Amazon. They’ve thought about it. They’ve maybe started the seller account setup. They haven’t finished.

This post isn’t going to argue that Amazon is worth it. That decision is usually already made. Instead, it’s going to put a number on what the delay actually costs — because most brand owners underestimate it significantly.

The 30% you’ve never counted

Start with this: 30% of Australian product searches start on Amazon, not Google.

Not Instagram. Not a comparison site. Amazon.

These aren’t people who chose another brand over yours after visiting your store. They never made it to your store. They opened Amazon, typed what they wanted, and bought from whoever showed up.

If your brand isn’t on Amazon, you don’t exist for this group. They’re not your bounced traffic or your abandoned carts. They’re an entirely invisible segment — buyers who have no awareness of you because you have no presence where they shop.

The Australian market leans further into this than many founders expect. Amazon AU had 60% buyer penetration in 2025. Two in three Amazon AU shoppers are Prime members — meaning they’ve pre-committed to buying from the platform. They pay for the privilege of buying from Amazon. If you’re not there, you’re not even in the conversation.

Section 1: The $6,700 myth

Most Shopify brands that haven’t launched on Amazon cite the same reason: “I’ll do it myself when I have time.”

Here’s what that project actually looks like when you break it down.

What “doing it yourself” involves Time / Out-of-pocket cost
Seller account setup and verification $200 + 3 hours
Catalogue build (GTIN sourcing, compliance mapping, category setup) 8–12 hours
Listing copy — written to Amazon’s SEO standards (per SKU, outsourced) $400–$600 per SKU
Photography reformatting to Amazon’s image specifications $200
First 90 days of trial, error, PPC learning, and rework $1,500–$3,000

Total: most brands land between $5,000 and $8,000 before making their first dollar. The $6,700 midpoint is not a scare figure — it’s what the average Shopify brand spends when they go in without a roadmap.

And that assumes you don’t make common mistakes that set you back further: listing under the wrong category, missing ARTG compliance requirements if you’re in health or beauty, or getting your account flagged during the verification process.

The time cost is the one that usually surprises people. Most brand owners aren’t Amazon experts. The learning curve for a channel you’ve never operated is real, and it comes out of time that would otherwise go into running the business you already have.

Section 2: The review compounding problem

The real cost of delay isn’t just the DIY overhead. It’s the reviews you’re not building.

Amazon’s algorithm is a compounding flywheel. Reviews drive ranking. Ranking drives visibility. Visibility drives sales, which drive more reviews. The brands sitting in the top 3 positions in your category didn’t get there because they had better products — they got there because they started earlier.

Consider two brands in the same category:

Brand A Brand B
Launch date January 2024 January 2026
Reviews by January 2026 180–220 8–15
Organic ranking (typical) Top 3 Page 2–3
PPC cost per click Lower (quality score) Higher (no history)

Brand A’s advantage isn’t permanent — Brand B can close the gap — but it takes 12–18 months of consistent volume to do it. Every month of delay extends that recovery timeline.

The compounding effect is why launch timing matters more on Amazon than on most channels. A day-one advantage compounds forward. A two-year delay compounds backward.

Section 3: What “managed launch” actually means

There’s a reason most Shopify brands that go it alone don’t get past the account setup. The Amazon ecosystem — FBA logistics, listing compliance, review strategy, PPC mechanics — is a separate skill set from running a Shopify store. It’s not complicated, but it requires time, repetition, and channel-specific knowledge.

A managed launch collapses that learning curve. Here’s what the three phases look like in practice:

Phase Months What’s happening
Launch Month 1 Account setup, catalogue build, listings live, FBA or FBM configured
Grow Months 2–3 Review velocity strategy, pricing optimisation, first ad spend
Compound Month 4+ Search rank improvement, catalogue expansion, cross-marketplace (eBay AU, Catch)

Month 1 is delivery. Month 2 onward is compounding. The distinction matters because brands who treat Amazon as a setup task — list it and leave it — consistently underperform brands who treat it as a managed channel.

The reviews don’t arrive automatically. The ranking doesn’t improve without optimisation. The catalogue doesn’t expand itself. This is what ongoing management buys: the work that converts a live listing into a profitable channel.
